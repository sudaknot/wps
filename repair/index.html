Piano Repair

Thoughtful solutions to keep your piano playing.

From individual broken parts to issues that affect how an entire piano plays, many problems can be addressed through careful repair and adjustment.

I'll take the time to understand what's going on, explain the available options, and help determine an appropriate solution for your instrument.

Examples of Repair Work

Pianos contain many individual components, and problems can arise in different areas of the instrument. Here are a few examples of repairs that may be needed.

01

String Splicing & Replacement

Piano strings can break for a variety of reasons, including age, corrosion, and the stresses of normal use.

Depending on the location of the break and the condition of the string, a splice may be an option. This involves joining the broken string with an additional section of wire to restore its function.

In other situations, replacing the string may be the more appropriate solution. The best approach depends on the individual circumstances.

02

Action & Mechanical Repairs

The piano action contains many moving parts that work together every time a key is played. Individual components can wear, become damaged, or stop functioning properly.

Repairs may include replacing broken hammer shanks, repairing or replacing worn springs, repinning action centers, and addressing other mechanical issues.

These repairs help restore proper movement and function to the affected components.

03

Key & Keyboard Repairs

Keyboard issues can range from loose or damaged keytops to keys that stick, wobble, or fail to return properly.

Depending on the issue, repairs may involve rebushing keys, replacing damaged keytops, or correcting mechanical problems affecting key movement.

The goal is to restore smooth, consistent operation across the keyboard.

Other Repairs & Maintenance

Not every piano problem fits neatly into one category. I also address a variety of other issues involving the action, keyboard, strings, and related components.

If something isn't working as it should, feel free to reach out. I can help determine whether it's something I can address and what the next steps might look like.

More Than One Way to Solve a Problem

Not every repair has just one possible solution. Depending on the problem, there may be several ways to approach it, each with different considerations in terms of cost, longevity, and the amount of work involved.

Sometimes a straightforward repair is all that's needed. Other times, replacing a component or taking a more extensive approach makes better sense.

I'll explain the options when they're available, along with the advantages and limitations of each. My goal is to help you make a decision that makes sense for both the piano and your expectations for it.

Understanding What Your Piano Needs

Before recommending a repair, it's important to understand the condition of the instrument and the nature of the problem.

Some issues are relatively simple to identify and address, while others may require a closer examination to determine their underlying cause.

I'll explain what I find, discuss any appropriate repair options, and provide an estimate based on the work involved before proceeding.

Not every repair is necessarily worth undertaking. The piano's overall condition, intended use, and the owner's goals all matter when deciding how to move forward.

Something Not Working Quite Right?

Whether it's a broken string, a sticking key, or another issue with your piano, I'd be happy to help you figure out what's going on.
