Piano Regulation

A well-adjusted action makes all the difference.

A piano's action is made up of hundreds of moving parts that work together to translate the movement of your fingers into sound. Over time, these parts can wear, shift, or fall out of adjustment.

Regulation involves carefully adjusting these components and their relationships to restore proper function, improve consistency, and help the piano respond as it should.

What Does Regulation Accomplish?

Regulation addresses the mechanical adjustments that determine how a piano feels and responds when played.

When a piano is properly regulated, its keys and action components work together in a coordinated and predictable way. This helps create a more consistent playing experience across the keyboard.

Depending on the piano and its condition, regulation may involve adjustments to key height and dip, hammer alignment and travel, let-off, checking, repetition, damper timing, and other mechanical relationships.

The goal is not simply to make adjustments for the sake of precision. It's to help the instrument perform more consistently and reliably for the person playing it.

Signs Your Piano May Need Regulation

Changes in how a piano feels and responds can indicate that its action needs attention.

Uneven Keys

Some keys sit higher or lower than others, or have noticeably different amounts of movement when played.

Inconsistent Response

Certain notes respond differently from others, making it difficult to achieve consistent control and expression.

Sluggish or Uneven Repetition

Keys or notes may not respond as quickly or reliably as expected when played repeatedly.

Uneven Touch

Some keys may feel heavier, lighter, or less predictable than others.

Damper Issues

Dampers may lift unevenly, respond inconsistently, or fail to stop notes as they should.

General Wear

Years of playing and normal wear can gradually affect the alignment and adjustment of the action.

These symptoms can have different causes, and not every playing issue requires regulation. An evaluation helps determine what adjustments or repairs may be appropriate.

Every Piano Is Different

The amount of regulation a piano needs depends on its age, condition, construction, and history of maintenance.

Some instruments may benefit from a few targeted adjustments, while others may need more extensive work throughout the action. In some cases, worn or damaged parts need to be addressed before proper regulation can be completed.
