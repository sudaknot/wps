The Importance of Tuning

More Than Just the Right Notes

A piano has hundreds of strings, each held under significant tension. Over time, changes in humidity, temperature, and regular use cause those strings to shift, gradually moving the piano away from its intended pitch.

Regular tuning helps maintain a consistent sound, keeps the instrument enjoyable to play, and can prevent larger pitch corrections from becoming necessary.

A Regular Schedule

How Often Should a Piano Be Tuned?

Most home pianos benefit from regular tuning, but the ideal schedule depends on how often the piano is played, its environment, and how well it holds its tuning.

01

Once a Year

A good starting point for many household pianos that receive moderate use. Annual tuning helps keep the instrument reasonably stable and enjoyable to play.

02

Twice a Year

Worth considering for frequently played pianos, instruments in changing environments, or pianos that tend to drift noticeably between tunings.

03

More Frequent Attention

Pianos used for lessons, performances, or other demanding situations may benefit from tuning several times throughout the year.

Your Piano's Environment

Humidity and Seasonal Changes

Seasonal changes in humidity are one of the biggest factors affecting a piano's tuning. The wooden components of a piano expand and contract as humidity changes, affecting string tension and overall pitch.

Maintaining a relatively consistent humidity level can help reduce these fluctuations. Keeping the piano away from heating vents, exterior doors, direct sunlight, and other sources of temperature changes can also help.
