What Does an Inspection Include?

A closer look at the piano's condition, from its structural components to the mechanism that produces its sound.

Soundboard & Bridges

Examining the soundboard and bridges for visible cracks, separation, damage, and other concerns that may affect the instrument.

Pinblock & Tuning Pins

Looking for visible signs of deterioration and evaluating potential concerns with tuning stability.

Plate & Strings

Checking for visible structural concerns, corrosion, damaged strings, and other signs of wear.

Action & Keyboard

Evaluating the condition and function of the piano's action, keys, and related mechanical components.

Cabinet & Overall Condition

Considering the general condition of the instrument, including its cabinet and other visible areas.

Additional Concerns

Identifying other issues that may warrant attention, further evaluation, or repair.

Inspections are based on the components and areas accessible at the time of the appointment. Some conditions may require additional disassembly or specialized evaluation to determine their full extent.

A Written Evaluation

An inspection is about more than identifying individual problems. It's about understanding the piano as a whole.

Following the inspection, you'll receive a written summary of my findings, including any concerns, recommended work, and areas that may need further attention.

Whether you're deciding if a piano is worth purchasing or trying to determine what to do with an instrument you already own, my goal is to give you the information you need to make an informed decision.

A Lesson I Learned Firsthand

Years ago, I acquired a piano without really knowing what I was getting into. I knew it needed some work, but I didn't realize it had a broken bridge and ultimately wasn't even tunable.

It was a lesson in how important it is to understand an instrument's condition before investing time and money into it.

Sometimes a piano is worth restoring, sometimes it needs a little attention, and sometimes the most helpful advice is knowing when not to invest further.

That's the kind of honest perspective I want to provide through every inspection.
